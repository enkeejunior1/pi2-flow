TL;DR

\(\pi\)-Flow trains its student against a teacher queried at states the teacher has never seen. \(\pi^2\)-Flow lengthens jumps progressively, so every teacher query stays where the teacher is reliable.

Flow-map distillation such as \(\pi\)-Flow keeps the diversity of a video diffusion teacher but trails distribution matching (DMD) in quality, most of all at 2 NFE. We trace the gap to state-dependent teacher reliability: \(\pi\)-Flow queries the teacher at states its own rollout reaches, and long jumps carry those states out of distribution.

\(\pi^2\)-Flow learns a 4-NFE policy with a short-to-long jump curriculum, then distills its trajectories into 2 NFE without querying the teacher at student states. On Wan2.1-1.3B it has the highest VBench score at 4 NFE (85.22) and 2 NFE (84.56), and stays more diverse than DMD.

Design principle

\(\pi\)-Flow is on-policy: the student rolls out its own policy and is supervised by the teacher at the state it reaches. A diffusion teacher is reliable only near its own trajectories, and a velocity error moves the reached state in proportion to the jump length. So the longer the jump, the less the teacher can be trusted. This gives one principle: keep the student’s queries near the teacher’s trajectories.

Method: lengthen jumps progressively

Stage 1 trains a 4-NFE policy with on-policy \(\pi\)-Flow, opening short jumps first. Stage 2 distills its trajectories into a 2-NFE student, whose single jump from noise would be too long to query the teacher safely.

Details

Stage 1

Guided initialization. The teacher is strongly guided (\(w = 9\)), while the student starts from the unguided base model. Absorbing the guidance alone takes about 256 updates, so we first distill the guided teacher into the student (KD) before Stage 1.

\(\Delta\sigma\) action weighting. We supervise the displacement over each grid interval rather than the raw velocity, which scales the residual by the interval width \(\Delta\sigma\). Under the shifted noise schedule, intervals widen toward the clean end of each segment, so the long jumps that the curriculum visits less often get commensurate weight. In the final segment, \(\Delta\sigma\) becomes too large, so there we sample query levels uniformly with unit weights.

Removing either KD or the curriculum with \(\Delta\sigma\) weighting costs 0.7 to 1.0 VBench points at 4 NFE (Ablations).

Stage 2

A fixed set of trajectories. Sampling fresh prompt–noise pairs at every update makes the Stage 2 loss plateau early. Precomputing the expert trajectories for a fixed set of 64 pairs and taking full-batch updates on them lets the loss keep falling, and raises 2-NFE VBench from 83.51 to 84.56 on unseen prompts, with a moderate cost in diversity.

Results on Wan2.1-1.3B and 14B

On Wan2.1-1.3B, \(\pi^2\)-Flow has the highest VBench score at both 4 NFE (85.22) and 2 NFE (84.56), about one point above our reproduction of the \(\pi\)-Flow-based PDD at each. On Wan2.1-14B it is competitive with the DMD-based methods but not ahead: close to rCM and below AnyFlow at 4 NFE, and below both at 2 NFE, while having the highest Quality score and dynamic degree in both settings.
